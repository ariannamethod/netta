# Turn24 RAW — the bytes actually paid at each oracle switch

Every number here is read back out of the traces pinned in
RESULTS_MANIFEST.json and recomputed independently by verify.py.
`cold` is P0, `candidate` is the archive quote, `live` is what the arm
charged; all are log2 probabilities, so less negative is cheaper.
`carried` is the arm's own odds arriving at the byte, before any clamp.
The seam is byte 8192 on every switched-law life, and the switch byte
is 8192+d.

## What the windows measure

Two facts, both computed from the tables above rather than read off them by eye.


**The slow arm arrives above the fast one at every switch, and never below it.** Across all 8 worlds and all 4 delays — 32 switches — `carried - clamp` is positive every time.


| d | mean | min | max | switches with carried > clamp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 6.0498 | 6.0049 | 6.1317 | 8/8 |
| 32 | 6.0618 | 6.0079 | 6.1287 | 8/8 |
| 128 | 6.1267 | 6.0194 | 6.2388 | 8/8 |
| 512 | 6.3476 | 6.0032 | 6.7058 | 8/8 |

The spread stays narrow: every one of the 32 values lies between 6.0032 and 6.7058, within 0.71 bit of log2(2^-10 / 2^-16) = 6, the log-ratio of the two hazards the arms run.


**Handing that stock back costs, in mean, at every delay.** Comparing the two laws over the stretch each one actually runs — byte 8192+d to the end of the life — O-level-d minus O-rate-d:


| d | mean gain difference (bits) | worlds where the clamp helped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | -0.2742 | 2/8 |
| 32 | -0.3581 | 2/8 |
| 128 | -1.2141 | 1/8 |
| 512 | -1.5175 | 3/8 |

That is the whole of W3 in one table. The level clamp is the strongest response available to a mechanism that cannot refund already-charged bytes, and on this batch it is still a net cost against simply changing the hazard rate: it discards roughly six bits of odds that the rate response keeps and spends down more gradually.
