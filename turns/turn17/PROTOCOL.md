## One question and one construction

Can ongoing authority use *previously observed* candidate evidence to retain
useful memory after a surface move without losing fast protection when the
underlying law changes? The candidate and portable 528-byte source archive
remain exactly turn13's joint-prefix construction. The local P0, HEAD256
frontend, source generator, first shadow-32 admission and episode candidate
are unchanged. Only the post-admission source-to-cold hazard varies.

The tested `adaptive` mode has one signed, decaying evidence state `e`,
initially zero. On an active quote at time `t`, use the **old** `e`:

    h_t = 2^-16 if e >= 1 bit, otherwise 2^-10.
    Q_t(y) = (C_t(y) + 2^z S_t(y)) / (1 + 2^z).

After the actual byte is charged, set `delta_t = log2(S_t(y)/C_t(y))` and
update source/cold odds with exactly the one-way law used by turn16, using
`h_t`. Only then update `e <- (255/256)e + delta_t` for the next byte.
The first-admission crossing observation does not enter `e`. Inactive quotes
are C. Equal C/S prices quote exact C. No future byte, world label, regime,
surface map or seam is supplied to authority. No reset or second admission
exists. The one-bit evidence threshold and 256-byte decay are fixed here;
neither is scanned on these worlds.

Paired controls are ordinary `slow` (h=2^-16) and `fast` (h=2^-10), both
with the same 1/2 initial source mass. All three receive identical C/S
prices and initial admission. This is an episode-arm authority experiment;
the inherited independent reader still verifies all seven candidate arms.
`row` and `permuted` remain visible controls but do not acquire this new
authority state. The portable archive size remains at most 528 bytes.

Because the initial cold path has mass 1/2, complete-prefix gain is at least
-1 bit. Each active update has hazard at least 2^-16, so interval drawdown
is at most 16 bits. These are arithmetic bounds, not performance claims.

rate search, rule edit or gate change follows the result.

## Material gate, all conditions required

Measurements use the episode arm, paired worlds and log-likelihood savings
relative to the same local P0. Ties are not wins.

1. On final-8192 moved-surface bytes, adaptive exceeds fast by **more than
   1 bit in mean** and wins **at least 5/8**; it trails slow by **at most
   3 bits in mean**.
2. On final-8192 switched-law bytes, adaptive trails fast by **at most
   1 bit in mean**, exceeds slow by **more than 1 bit in mean** and wins
   **at least 5/8** against slow.
3. On unchanged recombined lives, adaptive retains **at least 95%** of
   slow's positive mean early-4096 and full-life gains, and full-life gain
   is positive in **8/8**.
4. Episode first-admission bytes are identical in all modes. Episode admits
   on no unrelated life; source archive cap is respected. All quoted
   distributions are positive, normalized, and protected equal/NEW prices
   quote exactly P0. Prefix and interval bounds above hold on every life.
5. An independently written Decimal mass reader reconstructs source books,
   all seven inherited candidates, the surface bijection, every prospective
   episode authority quote/state/hazard and all gate fields within 1e-7
   bit. HEAD256 bindings and P0 remain explicitly supplied trace inputs,
   as in turns13–16.

Report every world, regime and authority: early, whole-life and changed-tail
gain, admission, minimum prefix, drawdown and slow-clock/fast-clock counts.
Show raw helpful, harmful and equal-price bytes with their charged costs.
